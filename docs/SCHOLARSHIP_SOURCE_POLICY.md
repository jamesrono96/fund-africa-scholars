# Scholarship source curation and verification policy

This policy defines how the owner and scholarship reviewers should select sources and validate opportunities. It is not a claim that a source has already been approved or that a current scholarship has been verified.

## Source acceptance

Prioritize, in order:

1. Official university and institution scholarship/admissions pages.
2. Government ministries, official national scholarship agencies and public education portals.
3. Official embassy or high-commission education and scholarship pages.
4. Official foundations and charitable scholarship providers.
5. Recognized scholarship portals only when the opportunity links to the actual provider's official application route.

Do not treat social-media posts, forwarded messages, scraped aggregators, blogs or third-party advertisements as verification evidence. They may point a reviewer toward a lead, but they cannot independently establish eligibility, funding or a deadline.

## Registration checks

Before registering a source in the admin dashboard, confirm:

- The organization exists and the domain is controlled by it or is its clearly documented official portal.
- The page uses HTTPS and is reachable without bypassing access controls.
- The page is relevant to international scholarships, fellowships or funded study.
- The source's geographic scope and applicant audience are understood.
- The reviewer can revisit the page and identify an official application route.

Record the institution name, exact official domain, scholarship page, source type, country and reviewer notes. Start with a conservative check interval (for example, 14 days); shorten it during an active application cycle if the provider updates frequently.

## Opportunity verification checklist

Before marking any record verified or published, a human reviewer must confirm and attach an official HTTPS evidence URL plus a concise excerpt for each applicable item:

1. Provider identity and official source.
2. Current intake is open or the opportunity is otherwise accepting applications.
3. Eligible nationalities, explicitly including the target African applicant countries.
4. Study level and programme eligibility.
5. Funding coverage, amounts, duration, conditions and costs not covered.
6. Application deadline, time zone and whether the deadline is fixed or rolling.
7. Official application route and required documents/tests.

A missing, ambiguous or conflicting item must remain in draft/needs-review. Never infer eligibility for African applicants from a general international-student statement. Never infer a deadline from a date-shaped string without checking its meaning on the official page.

## Re-check and expiry handling

- Review candidates flagged as changed before relying on old evidence.
- Re-check active records at least at the registered source interval and before prominent promotion.
- If a deadline has passed, confirm whether the provider has closed the intake, extended the deadline or opened a new cycle. A past date alone is not enough to silently archive a record.
- If an official page disappears or becomes inaccessible, mark the record for review and seek a current official source.
- Keep a clear source-checked date and retain reviewer decisions in the audit history.

## Discovery automation boundaries

Automated scans and optional AI extraction create leads and evidence suggestions only. They must not decide that an opportunity is genuine, that an African applicant is eligible, that funding is fully funded, or that a deadline is current. No automated process may mark a scholarship verified or publish it without the full human evidence checklist.

## Reviewer quality controls

- Use two-person review for high-value/high-risk records where staffing permits.
- Avoid copying full copyrighted provider pages; retain only short evidence excerpts and source links.
- Do not collect applicant identity documents or sensitive personal data in the scholarship directory.
- Record corrections and source changes transparently.
- Escalate suspicious providers, application fees paid to individuals, requests for passwords, or unofficial payment channels.

## Owner setup checklist

- [ ] Owner approves the initial source list and source priorities.
- [ ] Add sources manually through the admin dashboard after domain verification.
- [ ] Assign a named reviewer and backup reviewer.
- [ ] Agree on review cadence and how expired/changed records are handled.
- [ ] Test the evidence checklist using synthetic records before real publication.
